About FRAN

Fairfield Resilient Action Network

Our Mission

Fostering economic opportunity, social wellbeing, and environmental health through bottom-up community collaboration and partnerships.

Our Vision

A resilient Fairfield with strong food systems, quality housing, healthy land and water, reduced waste, a thriving local economy, and transparent, forward-thinking planning.

RAC → FRAN:  From Planning to Implementation

The Resilient Action Committee (RAC) was created as a volunteer advisory group to research community needs and develop Fairfield’s Resilient Action Plan.

As the planning phase concluded, it became clear that successful implementation would require dedicated coordination, fundraising capacity, and long-term partnerships.

The Fairfield Resilient Action Network (FRAN) was formed to carry this work forward, supporting implementation of the plan through coordinated projects, volunteer leadership, and collaboration with the City and community partners.

RAC to FRAN Origin Timeline

					
					 

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		March
		2023
  	
				
				
				
	
		Where We Started
		
		
Thirty community volunteers were guided and organized to form the Resilient Action Committee (RAC), a volunteer advisory group focused on Fairfield’s long-term resilience.

	
			
		

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		April – October
		2023
  	
				
				
				
	
		
		
		
The RAC self-organized into six subcommittees. Each group met monthly to research, discuss, and identify key challenges and opportunities in the community.

	
			
		

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		October – November
		2023
  	
				
				
				
	
		
		
		
The Resilient Action Plan (RAP) development framework was adopted. Fairfield hired Sustainability Coordinator to support the process.

	
			
		

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		December '23 – May '25
		2023 - 2025
  	
				
				
				
	
		
		
		
Subcommittees researched, collaborated, and drafted Fairfield’s first Resilient Action Plan, identifying priorities, objectives, and implementation pathways.

	
			
		

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		July '24 – March '25
		2024 - 2025
  	
				
				
				
	
		
		
		
The RAC supported the City in pursuing major state and federal housing and community development grants, while expanding public outreach and engagement.

	
			
		

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		May – June
		2025
  	
				
				
				
	
		
		
		
Each subcommittee prioritized early implementation projects. The draft Resilient Action Plan was presented to the City Council.

	
			
		

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		July 2025 – Now (Ongoing)
		2025/26
  	
				
				
				
	
		
		
		
Early implementation activities launched, including housing, food systems, waste reduction, and stewardship initiatives.

	
			
		

			
	
		  
	
			
				
	
		Fall 2025 - Where We Are Now
		2026
  	
				
				
				
	
		
		
		
FRAN was formally incorporated as an Iowa nonprofit, received its EIN, and received 501(c)(3) status to support independent fundraising, partnerships, and project coordination.

	
			
		

						
					
					
			

Staff & Board

Faith Reeves

Director / Project Manager

Faith Reeves serves as Fairfield’s Sustainability Coordinator and the founding Project Director of the Fairfield Resilient Action Network (FRAN). With over a decade of experience in community development, soil health, and local food systems, she specializes in translating community vision into practical, on-the-ground projects. Faith convened and facilitated the 35-member Resilient Action Committee and now leads Phase 2 implementation through partnerships, funding coordination, and volunteer-driven action.

Stuart Valentine

Board Chair

Stuart Valentine is a Fairfield-based leader with decades of experience in finance, sustainability, and nonprofit governance. He is Board Chair of the Sustainable Living Coalition and previously served on the board of the Sustainable Iowa Land Trust, supporting permanent protection of farmland for local food production. As a co-founder of Centered Wealth, Stuart brings a strong background in impact investing, strategic planning, and community-centered economic development to FRAN’s work.

Douglas S. Flournoy

Board Vice Chair

Doug Flournoy is a longtime Fairfield resident, educator, and civic leader with deep experience in public service. A retired chemistry professor at Indian Hills Community College, he has served on the Fairfield Community School District Board, including as president, and on the Fairfield City Council, where in his last term he served as Mayor Pro Tem. Doug brings a strong understanding of local governance, education, and community collaboration to FRAN’s board.

Don Hoelting

Board Member

Don Hoelting brings decades of experience in accounting, property management, and local volunteer leadership. A former CPA and controller for several Fairfield companies, he has also owned and managed a modular and manufactured home business and overseen residential renovations. Don has served on the boards of the Fairfield Art Walk and Fairfield Volunteer Center and contributes practical financial and operational insight to FRAN’s work.

Margaret Dwyer

Board Secretary

Marg Dwyer is a longtime Fairfield resident with a background spanning education, finance, and public service. After a 19-year management career at Cambridge Investment Research, she has focused her energy on community and environmental initiatives. Marg has served on the local school board, is active with the Southeast Iowa Sierra Club, and was part of the leadership team for the Resilient Fairfield Initiative. She now serves as FRAN’s board secretary.

Kristina Branstetter

Board Member

Kristina Branstetter is a Fairfield-based attorney and community leader committed to strengthening resilient, collaborative local systems. She serves as in-house counsel at Cambridge Investment Research, where she supports the organization through legal guidance and thoughtful governance. Kristina holds a Juris Doctor and an MBA and brings a strong background in law, business, and organizational leadership to her work. In addition to her professional role, she is actively engaged in community service, serving on the Main Street Fairfield Executive Board and chairs its Organization Committee, and she is also Chair-Elect of Cambridge’s Community Engagement Committee. She is proud to serve on the board of the Fairfield Resilient Action Network in support of a thriving Fairfield community.
